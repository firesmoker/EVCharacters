# Eternal Valor Playtest

Eternal Valor is a fantasy tabletop RPG system designed with the following principles:

## Combat

- Minimize players' downtime

  - Combat is fast, and the players all take their turns at the same time.

  - Minimum rolls, using d6s exclusively.

- Coordination and team playing are crucial for success.

- Combat is short and deadly. However, it is not meant to feel oppressive or over punishing.

- Strategy and deduction are central to combat.

- Magic is powerful and can change the tide of the battle but mostly requires team effort.

## Roleplay

- Players should feel powerful and capable - however deadly the combat is, or other situations are challenging, it is important that their characters are very good at fulfilling their fantasy.

- Characters should feel unique and different to each other. No single character should be too generalized. Each player needs to feel that their character has a place and role within the party.

- Social interactions should not be monopolized by a single character.

- All characters should feel useful both in and out of combat. Still, some may be more combat centric than others.

- Character statistics should be impactful.

- Character progression should be impactful.

- Support roles should be very satisfying and impactful. Healing, debuffing and buffing by support characters are very potent.

- Players and their characters are allies, with an overall shared goal. Being toxic or intentionally disruptive is discouraged.

- Characters are assumed to be Good, or at least their interests are aligned with Good intentions, unless all players agree not to in advance.

  - For example, even if you play a Witch or other “dark” character, you still align yourself with the party’s overall course of action. A helpful solution might be to find a reason for this course of action to be beneficial for your otherwise shady motivations.

## Exploration

- Loot, especially high-quality items, magical items and artifacts, should feel impactful.

# Chapter 1: Rolls

Rolls determine the outcome of your actions. When the GM calls for a roll, you roll three 6-sided dice. Throughout the book, it appears as 3d6.

Each die that results in a 4 or higher is a Success Die (also appears as SD). The total number of Success Dice determines the result.

### Meeting a Threshold

To succeed in a roll, you need to meet or surpass a minimum number of Success Dice. That number is called a threshold. If the number of Success Dice is equal to or higher than the threshold, you pass.


>
> John searches for a hidden lever. The GM calls for a roll with a threshold of 2.
>
> John rolls a 3, 4, and 6. Since 4 or higher is a Success Die, the 4 and 6 dice give him two successes—the minimum needed to meet the threshold. John finds the lever and opens the secret passage.

### Measuring Success?

For some rolls, the more Success Dice you get, the more powerful the effect is. Each action or spell will tell you exactly what each success adds to the result.


>
> Example: The Melee Strike Action
>
> When you strike an enemy with a weapon, the damage you deal is equal to the number of success dice you roll

## Double 6

For some rolls, if two or more dice land on a 6, an additional effect takes place.

## Extra Dice

Some effects may provide you with extra dice to throw. For example, if you would roll 4d6, but get 2 extra dice – you roll 6d6 instead.

## Penalties

Penalties reduce the number of successes you get. For each penalty you get one fewer success.


>
> For example: if you roll and get 3 Success Dice, and you have a penalty of 1, it changes your result as if you've rolled 2 Success Dice instead.

## Advantage and Disadvantage

### Advantage

Advantage is a benefit you can get from many different sources. It helps you succeed in rolls more easily. When you get advantage for a roll, you roll the dice twice and choose one result


>
> **Example**
> You roll 5d6 once and get:
> 1,2,3,3,4 (One Success Die)
> You roll 5d6 for a second time and get:
> 1,3,4,4,6 (Three Success Dice).
> You then decide to choose the second roll, as it has more Success Dice.

### Disadvantage

Disadvantage is the opposite of Advantage.

If any effect or circumstance provides you with a disadvantage for a roll, you roll the dice twice but must choose the result with less Success Dice.

### Advantage and Disadvantage Together

If you are provided with both advantage and disadvantage, you roll the dice only once, as if you had no advantage or disadvantage. It does not matter how many sources give you advantage or disadvantage.

Important:

You only use the highest level of training you have for each skill. If multiple sources (such as your class, species, or origin) grant you training in the same skill, they do not stack.

For example: If your class provides you with Expert (5d6) training in Athletics, and your species provides you with Adept (4d6) training in Athletics, you are only considered Expert (5d6) in Athletics. When you roll with Athletics, you roll 5d6.

## Skill Types

Skills have three types: Combat skills, Standard skills, and Crafting skills. Your class, species, origin, and other sources provide you with training in different skills.

### Standard Skills

Various useful skills. There are four categories: Speech, Knowledge, Perception, and Other. See the Standard Skills table later in this chapter.

### Combat Skills

Combat skills are used for combat actions but can sometimes be used outside of it.

They include three categories: Weapons, Magic, and Unarmed.

See the Combat Skills table later in this chapter.

### Crafting Skills

Skills that enable the creation of different items. They may require other skills to function and unlock different options (described in the “Crafting” chapter).

See the Crafting Skills table later in this chapter.

## Conditional Skill Training

Certain species, classes, or other factors may provide you with training in skills that only apply in specific conditions. It looks like this:

Skill (Condition)

For example, the Rogue class starts as Adept (4d6) in Mechanical (Disarm Traps). That means you roll 4d6 instead of 3d6 only when you use the skill to disarm traps, and not for other purposes, such as building a mechanism.

# Chapter 3: Combat

Combat is divided into rounds.

## Round Structure

Each round goes through three phases in the following order: Planning Phase, Beginning Phase, and the Action Phase.

### 1. Planning Phase

All actors (players and non-player characters or NPCs) commit to a single Action and its target (or targets) for the round. A target can be another actor or a point, depending on the action.

### 2. Beginning Phase

Any effect that happens or needs to be activated “at the beginning phase” occurs now.

### 3. Action Phase

Now, it’s time for the actions to resolve in the following order:

#### Fast Actions Resolution

1. Fast actions and movements by heroes (and their allies) resolve one hero at a time.<br>The players choose the order in which to play them.

2. Fast actions and movements by the enemy NPCs resolve one NPC at a time.<br>The GM chooses the order in which to play them.

#### Average Actions Resolution

1. Average actions and movements by heroes and their allies resolve one at a time.<br>The players choose the order in which to play them.

2. Average Actions and movements by the enemy NPCs resolve one at a time.<br>The GM chooses the order in which to play them.

#### Slow Actions Resolution

1. Slow actions and movements by heroes and their allies resolve one at a time.<br>The players choose the order in which to play them.

3. Slow actions and movements by the enemy NPCs resolve one at a time.<br>The GM chooses the order in which to play them.

### Action Validity at Resolution

- If an actor’s intended action becomes impossible by the time it is resolved, the action is usually canceled.

- On the contrary, if an actor’s intended action becomes possible by the time it is resolved, the action goes through.


>
> You declared that you would shoot an arrow at the goblin who is currently hidden behind a wall (an impossible action). If, by the time your action is resolved, the goblin has moved out from behind the wall and is now visible, you proceed with the shot—the action is now possible and goes through.

## Combat Movement

On each round, right before or right after you perform your action, you may move up to your Movement score.

Notice: You don’t need to commit to whether you move (or where) during the Planning Phase.

### Actions that Use Movement

- Some actions include movement as part of their effect (e.g., a charge or dash).

- If you take one of these actions, you cannot move separately in the same round.

- Your movement is limited to what is described in the action itself.

### Special Cases

- You may move through an ally’s space.

- Moving over difficult terrain takes twice as much movement. (Each unit square that normally costs 1 movement costs 2).

## Instinct

Characters and NPCs sometimes have a resource called "Instinct". This allows them to activate certain abilities at different timings throughout the round. There is no need to commit to Instinct abilities during the Planning Phase.

Instinct resets at the beginning of each round for each character, according to their Instinct score.

## Attack and Defense

### Defenses

Defenses measure a character's ability to resist or withstand various types of threats and attacks. There are three types of defenses:

#### Deflection

Deflection represents a character's ability to deflect or avoid attacks, such as through natural agility, using a shield, dodging, etc.

#### Fortitude

Fortitude represents a character's physical resilience and toughness—their ability to withstand physical harm, resist poison, disease, and endure harsh conditions.

#### Will

Will represents a character's mental strength and resolve: their ability to resist fear, manipulation, mental attacks, and other effects that target the mind.

### Attacks

Most offensive actions - such as strikes and some spells - target a specific defense.

The defense is applied as a penalty for the roll. This is written as:

Roll (Skill) against (Defense).

Examples:

- Roll Melee against Deflection

- Roll Athletics against Fortitude

- Roll Magic against Will


>
> A warrior performs a Melee Strike against a goblin captain. The description says, "Roll Melee against Deflection". The warrior is Expert (5d6) with Melee, so they roll 5d6. He gets 2 Success Dice. The goblin captain’s Deflection is 1, so the warrior suffers a penalty of 1. The warrior result is reduced from 2 Success Dice to 1 Success Die.

#### Attacks With a Threshold

Some attack descriptions require you to meet or pass a threshold for the full effect.

For example: Roll Magic against Deflection. The threshold is 2.


>
> A witch casts a "Bestow Curse" spell on a paladin. The spell says: "Roll Magic against Will. The threshold is 1."
>
> The witch rolls 5d6 and gets 2 Success Dice. Because the paladin’s Will is 1, the witch subtracts 1 from her successes. Her total is reduced from 2 Success Dice to 1 Success Die. This is enough to meet the threshold of 1, so the spell succeeds.

#### Rolling Against Multiple Targets

If the attack is done against multiple targets, the attacker still only rolls once, but the penalty is different for each affected target, according to their own defense. These attacks often have a threshold for the full effect.

## Melee Reach

Whenever "Melee Reach" is mentioned, it means 1 adjacent unit to the character, unless specified otherwise (such as with "Reach" weapons).

## Melee vs. Ranged Attacks

Many actions, spells, and effects mention the type of attacks. The four types of attacks are: Melee Attack, Ranged Attack, Melee Spell Attack, Ranged Spell Attack.
Performing Ranged Attacks and Ranged Spell Attacks while being in an enemy Melee Reach has disadvantage.

## Damage

Damage deducts Health Points (HP) from the receiver by the amount of damage they receive.

### Temporary Hit Points

Some effects may provide a character with temporary hit points. When a character takes damage and has temporary hit points, they are lost first, and any leftover damage carries over to the normal hit points.

## Reaching 0 Hit Points

When an enemy drops to 0 HP, they are dead.

When you drop to 0 HP, you are dying. You are unconscious and prone. You also get one level of Exhaustion (described later).

At the end of each round, roll 3d6. The threshold is 2. Note each time you succeed or fail. Roll until you stabilize or die.

- Two successes: You stabilize.

- Two failures: You die.

If combat ends before you stabilize or die, keep rolling once per minute until you do.

If you receive additional damage while you are dying, you suffer one failure. If the damage is larger than your maximum HP, you die immediately.

If you receive healing or an ally successfully performs the Stabilize action on you, you become stabilized.

### Becoming Stabilized

When you become stabilized you stop dying. You remain unconscious until the end of combat, even if you are healed. You must have at least 1 HP to become conscious again.

## Exhaustion

You get +1 Drag (explained in the 'Actions' chapter) to all actions for each level of Exhaustion you get. When you get to 3 levels of Exhaustion, you die.

Exhaustion mainly occurs by dying and extreme environmental or magical effects.

1 level of Exhaustion is removed on each long rest.

## Interruption

Some actions, most commonly spellcasting, can be interrupted. An interruption occurs if:

- An effect specifically states it interrupts, or

- The actor takes damage while performing an interruptible action. A total damage of 2 in the same round, from one source or more, is required.


>
> Example
>
> A witch is casting the Summon Shade spell (a Slow, interruptible action). An enemy archer hits her for 1 damage. If she takes at least 1 more damage until the end of the round, her spell will be cancelled.

# Chapter 4: Actions

## Action Speed

Actions come in four speed levels: Fast, Average, Slow and (rarely) Free[comment 1].

Generally, slower actions have higher impact, such as casting powerful spells.

You can choose to perform an action at a slower speed. (Perform a Fast action as an Average or Slow action, etc.)

## Drag

Drag is a modifier that slows an action by one speed level. If Drag would reduce the action below Slow, the action is cancelled and cannot be performed.


>
> Example
>
> Push is a Fast Action by default.
>
> With 1 Drag, it becomes Average Action.
>
> With 2 Drag, it becomes Slow Action.
>
> With 3 or more Drag, it becomes too slow to perform and is cancelled.

Sources of Drag include:

- Weapon Drag Properties (such as Heavy, Ranged)

- Special effects (such as Bestow Curse spell)

Some features allow you to ignore different sources of Drag. Many player character classes have a “Drags Ignored” section that describes which sources of drags are ignored[comment 2], effectively helping you to perform actions at faster speeds.

## Actions that Use Movement

Some actions have the "Uses Movement" tag. When you commit to such action, you cannot choose to move before or after the action. Instead, the movement is part of the action, and you may only perform it according to the action's description.

## Action Descriptions

### Creative Action

Varying Speed
Perform an action not described here or in other relevant places to your character. The GM will decide on its feasibility, its relevant skill (if any), and the required threshold to succeed. The GM may also decide that it can be performed for free together with another action.

### Bomb Throw

Average Action
Effective Range: 3 units
Maximum Range: 6 units
Throw a bomb at target location. If you throw the bomb within the effective range, it lands in place. If you throw it between the effective and maximum range, roll Bomb Throw. The threshold is 2. On failure, the bomb lands in another location within range. The GM can choose the location or roll for a random result.
Follow the bomb's description for its effects and area when it lands.
If an enemy is within 1 unit of you, the effective range is 1, and the maximum range is equal to your normal effective range.

### Cast a Cantrip

Varying Action Speed
Cast a known cantrip (Level 0 spell) according to its description.

### Cast a Spell

Varying Action Speed
Cast a spell according to its description.
You must spend Spell Points as required in the spell description to do so. You may also choose to spend more spell points for greater effects, as described in the spell description.
Notice that many spells are Slow Action and interruptible. If your casting is interrupted, you don't lose the spent spell points.

### Charge

Average Action ◆ Uses movement
Double your Movement score for this round and move to the chosen enemy. You must finish your movement in a place where that enemy is within your melee reach. Then, perform a Melee Strike or Unarmed Strike on that enemy.
Even if moving to your enemy is no longer possible when your action resolves, you must move as close as possible.

### Long Charge

You may instead perform Charge as a Slow Action, tripling your Movement instead of doubling it.

### Dash

Fast Action ◆ Uses movement
Double your Movement score for this round and move.

### Long Dash

You may instead perform Dash as an Average Action, tripling your Movement instead of doubling it.

### Grapple

Fast Action
You grapple an enemy that is adjacent to you. Roll Unarmed against Deflection or Fortitude, whichever is higher. The target’s size must be up to one size larger than you. On success, you grapple the target. See the "Glossary" chapter for grappling rules.

### Melee Strike

Fast Action
You strike an enemy within your melee range with a melee weapon. Roll Melee against Deflection. Deal 1 damage for each Success Die + any weapon damage bonuses.
The damage type is appropriate to the weapon.
On success, if you rolled a double six, activate the weapon's double six ability.

### Push

Fast Action
You push a target that is adjacent to you. Roll Athletics against Deflection or Fortitude (whichever is higher). The threshold is 1. The target’s size must be up to one size larger than you.
On success - push target 1 unit away from you and the target is interrupted (if applicable).

### Ranged Strike

Fast Action
Shoot a target using a ranged weapon.
Roll Ranged against Deflection.
On success, deal SD + any weapon damage bonuses.
The damage type is appropriate to the weapon. On success, use the weapon-specific Double-6 ability.
Notice that ranged weapons have the "Ranged" property, which introduces 1 Drag to Ranged Strike, making it an Average Action.
You must have a feature that lets you ignore this Drag to be able to perform Ranged Strike as a Fast Action.

### Stabilize

Slow Action
You stabilize a dying creature that is adjacent to you.
Roll Medicine. Add 1 death roll success for each success die.
If you roll 3 SD or more, the target becomes conscious with 1 HP.
On success, if you rolled a double 6, suffers no exhaustion.

### Unarmed Strike

Fast Action
Strike a target within melee reach with one of your legs or hands.
Roll Unarmed against Deflection.
On success, deal SD bludgeoning damage.

### Utilize

Varying Speed
Varying roll types. May also succeed automatically.
Perform an action with an item or environmental feature according to their description or the GM’s ruling.

### Weapon Throw

Average Action
Using a weapon with the “thrown weapon” property, throw the weapon at a target within range.
Roll Weapon Throw against Deflection.
On success, deal SD damage + any weapon damage bonuses.
The damage type is appropriate to the weapon.
Modifiers:
You gain disadvantage on the roll if you are within melee reach of an enemy
You gain disadvantage if the range of the attack is higher than the weapon’s effective range (you cannot throw further than the maximum range).

### Dodge

Referenced in the Action Table, but no complete action description is present.

# Chapter 5: Player Characters

A player character is defined by 3 things:

1. Origin: what your character did before becoming what they are, and their prominent character traits.[comment 3][comment 4]

4. Species: their species, such as Human, Elf, or Dwarf.

5. Class: their profession, such as Warrior, Wizard, or Cleric.

When you build your character, you may choose them in any order you wish.

## Statistics Descriptions

#### Defenses

All the following statistics help the character to avoid and reduce the effectiveness of negative effects:

Armor Training: Armor the character can use. Armor adds to Deflection.

If you wear armor that you are not trained in, you get disadvantage on all rolls and 1 Drag to all actions.

Shield Training: Shields the class is allowed to use. Shield adds to the Deflection score.

#### Speed

All the following statistics help the character perform actions especially fast:

Drags Ignored: many sources add Drag to certain actions, making them slower. The Drag sources that appear in this section are ignored by this class, effectively making the character perform the related actions faster. For example:

Drags Ignored: Heavy Weapons

# Chapter 6: Species

## Human

### Human Traits

- Languages: Common, one other language of your choice

- Movement Score: 3

- Human Ingenuity: Choose one Feat that you satisfy the requirements for.

- Human Curiosity: Choose three standard skills. You are Adept (4d6) in each. You may not select more than one skill from the same category, except for the 'Other' category.

## Dwarf

### Dwarf Traits

- Languages: Common, Dwarven

- Movement Score: 3

- Powerful Build: You have additional HP equal to half your level, rounded up.

- Sturdy Form: If any effect makes you become prone, pushed, or pulled, roll 3d6 against difficulty 1. On success, you don't become prone, and you are pushed or pulled for 2 units less (to a minimum of 0).

- Subspecies: there are two main subspecies of dwarves: Surface Dwarf and Deep Dwarf. Choose one of these subraces.

### Subspecies

Choose one subspecies:

#### Surface Dwarf

- Between Two Worlds: You are Adept (4d6) in two standard skills of your choice, each from a different category.<br>You also know one additional language of your choice.

#### Deep Dwarf

- Resilience: You get +1 Fortitude

- Poison Resistance: You receive half poison damage, rounded down.

## Orc

### Orc Traits

# Chapter 9: Equipment

## Weapons

Any class can use any simple weapon. Martial weapons can only be used if you have proficiency with martial weapons (usually from a feat or a class feature)

Weapons have many properties with various effects. Some properties introduce Drag if a character attacks with them. They are called Drag Properties.

Certain classes and feats provide the ability to ignore the Drag introduced by certain Drag Properties.

Switching between weapons is free, unless it's between two 'Heavy' or 'Clumsy' weapons. In that case, a dedicated average action is required to switch between them.

### Weapon Properties

#### Drag Properties

- Clumsy: Switching into or from a ‘Clumsy’ weapon requires using the ‘Switch’ Instinct ability.

- Heavy: Switching into or from a ‘Heavy’ weapon requires using the ‘Switch’ Instinct ability.

- Ranged:

  - Requires both hands to use.

  - Attacks suffer disadvantage if you are within the melee reach of an enemy.

  - You can only attack targets up to the maximum range of the weapon.

  - Range appears as: Range [effective range] / [maximum range].

  - Attacks that target enemies who are between the effective range and the maximum range suffer disadvantage.

- Reach: Melee reach of 2 units. Attacks against targets within 1 unit suffer disadvantage.

- Two Handed: Requires both hands to use.

#### Other Properties

- Exotic: cannot be used without specialized training (usually through Exotic Weapons feat)

- Loading: Only exists for Ranged weapons. Attacks suffer 1 additional Drag except the first attack in a combat.

- Mounted: Advantage when attacking while mounted.

- Reliable: Rolling 1 SD with Strikes with this weapon count as 2 SD.

- Small: Switching into a ‘Small’ weapon doesn’t require any Instinct ability, even from a ‘Heavy’ or ‘Clumsy’ weapon. Easier to hide.

- Versatile: Can be used both as a one-handed and two-handed weapon. If used as a two-handed weapon, the number after the Versatile damage represents the damage bonus. (If used as two-handed weapon Drag is introduced as any other two-handed weapon.)

#### Special Properties:

- Rapier: +1 damage if the second hand is free

- Flintlock: only uses one hand.

#### Double 6 Abilities

- Breach: The target is Breached 1 (-1 Deflection) until the end of the next round

- Disable: The target has disadvantage on its next attack until the end of the next round.
